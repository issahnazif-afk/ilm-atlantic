## Part A: Official formats

### A1. PTE Core (Pearson)

PTE Core is the PTE version IRCC accepts for Canadian economic immigration. Main sources:
- [V] Format pages: https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-core/test-format/speaking-writing, /reading, /listening
- [V] **PTE Core Test Taker Score Guide (2026)**: https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/PTE-Core-Score-Guide-2026.pdf
- [V] Pearson says **"PTE Core and PTE Home tests remain unchanged"** by the August 2025 update: https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-updates-2025/

**Overall [V] (Score Guide):**
- About 2 hours. The guide states "No test will exceed a total of 2 hours."
- Each test has **52–67 tasks** across **19 task types**.
- Overall score and four skill scores (Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing) are each reported on a **10–90** scale.
- Integrated tasks count towards two skills.
- The overall score is not the average of the skill scores.
- Scoring is automated, and the engine is trained on human ratings.
- If Content (or Form) scores 0, the whole item scores 0. Examples: an off-topic email, an email under 30 words, or a "significant amount of pre-prepared/memorized material".

#### Part 1: Speaking & Writing (50–65 min)

| Task | Items (Score Guide) | Prompt | Prep | Response / time limit | Skills | Traits scored |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal Introduction (unscored) | 1 | – | 25 s | 30 s | – | Not scored |
| Read Aloud | 6–7 | Text ≤60 words | 30–40 s | Varies with text length | Reading + Speaking | Content, Oral fluency, Pronunciation (partial credit) |
| Repeat Sentence | 10–12 | Audio 3–9 s | none | Format page shows "40 seconds". This is probably a page error, because PTE Academic lists 15 s for this task **[check]** | Listening + Speaking | Content, Fluency, Pronunciation |
| Describe Image | 3–4 | Image | 25 s | 40 s | Speaking | Content, Fluency, Pronunciation |
| **Respond to a Situation** | 2–4 | Text ≤60 words (read and heard) | 20 s, then 20 s after the audio | 40 s | Speaking | **Appropriacy (0–3)**, Fluency (0–5), Pronunciation (0–5) |
| Answer Short Question | 5–6 | Audio 3–9 s | none | 10 s (recording stops after 3 s of silence) | Listening + Speaking | Vocabulary, scored correct/incorrect |
| Summarize Written Text | 1–2 | Text ≤200 words | – | 10 min | Reading + Writing | Content (0–2), Form (2 = **25–50 words**; 1 = 5–24 or 51–60), Grammar, Vocabulary |
| **Write Email** | 2–3 | Text ≤100 words | – | **9 min** | Writing | Content (0–3), **Email conventions**, Form (2 = **50–120 words**; 1 = 30–49 or 121–140; 0 = <30 or >140), Organization, Vocabulary range, Grammar, Spelling |

Note: the Write Email page gives conflicting length advice, "between 80 and 120 words" in the how-to section and "50–120" in the scoring section. The Score Guide's Form scale (50–120 words for full marks) is the scoring rule. Advising learners to aim for 80–120 words fits both.

#### Part 2: Reading (27–37 min)

| Task | Items | Text length | Format | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reading & Writing: Fill in the Blanks | 5–6 | ≤200 words | Drop-down gaps | Partial credit, per blank |
| Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers | 1–2 | ≤275 words | Select ≥1 option | +1 per correct option, −1 per wrong option, minimum 0 |
| Reorder Paragraph | 2–3 | ≤110 words | Drag boxes into order | +1 per correct adjacent pair |
| Fill in the Blanks | 4–5 | ≤80 words | Drag words from a bank (distractors included) | Per blank |
| Multiple Choice, Single Answer | 1–2 | ≤110 words | Choose one option | Correct/incorrect |

#### Part 3: Listening (22–37 min)

| Task | Items | Audio | Response | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Summarize Spoken Text | 1–2 | 45–75 s | Write **20–30 words** in 8 min | Content, Form (2 = 20–30 words; 1 = 5–19 or 31–40), Grammar, Vocabulary, Spelling |
| Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers | 1–2 | 50–90 s | Select ≥1 option | ±1 per option |
| Fill in the Blanks | 2–3 | 30–60 s | Type the missing words | Per word spelled correctly |
| Multiple Choice, Single Answer | 1–2 | 30–60 s | Choose one option | Correct/incorrect |
| Select Missing Word | 1–2 | 20–70 s | Choose from 3–5 options | Correct/incorrect |
| Highlight Incorrect Words | 1–2 | 20–70 s | Click words that differ from the audio | Per word (partial credit) |
| Write from Dictation | 3–4 | 3–5 s | Type the sentence | Per word spelled correctly |

Item counts come from the Score Guide tables, read by position from the PDF.

#### PTE Core compared with PTE Academic

| | PTE Core | PTE Academic (from 7 Aug 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Writing task | **Write Email** (9 min, 50–120 words) | **Write Essay** (20 min) |
| Respond to a Situation | Yes. Prep 20 s + 20 s, speak 40 s. Scored on **Appropriacy**/Fluency/Pronunciation | Yes, **new in Aug 2025**. Prep 10 s + 10 s, speak 40 s. Scored on Content/Pronunciation/Oral fluency |
| Summarize Group Discussion | **Absent** | **New in Aug 2025.** Audio ≤3 min, 10 s prep, 2 min speaking |
| Retell Lecture | **Absent** | Present: audio ≤90 s, 10 s prep, 40 s speaking |
| Summarize Written Text | Text ≤200 words; write 25–50 words in 10 min | Text ≤300 words; 10 min |
| Summarize Spoken Text | 20–30 words | 50–70 words **[U, my knowledge; check the Academic listening page]** |
| Content register | General / everyday | Academic |

### A2. PTE Academic 2025 update [V]

Source: https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-updates-2025/ and the news release https://www.pearsonpte.com/news/pearson-advances-english-language-assessment-with-enhanced-pte-test/ (announced 10 Jul 2025).

- **Effective 7 August 2025.** Applies to PTE Academic and PTE Academic UKVI only. PTE Core and PTE Home are unchanged.
- **Two new Speaking & Writing tasks:**
  - **Summarize Group Discussion**: audio up to 3 min (three speakers), **10 s prep**, **2 min** response. Scored on Content, Pronunciation, Oral Fluency.
  - **Respond to a Situation**: prompt text ≤60 words (read and heard), **10 s** to think plus **10 s** after the audio, **40 s** response. Scored on Content, Pronunciation, Oral fluency.
  - Timings are from https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic/test-format/speaking-writing.
- The original 20 task types and the sections stay the same. There are slightly more questions, but the test is still about 2 hours and per-question time limits are unchanged.
- Some responses are now **part-scored by humans as well as AI** to detect memorized or templated answers. Humans never score pronunciation or fluency.
- Same 10–90 score report and CEFR alignment. Pearson's 3 Scored Practice Tests now include the new tasks.
- Some Australian DHA score requirements changed on 7 Aug 2025 (Pearson links to a separate article).

### A3. CELPIP-General (Paragon Testing Enterprises; materials now carry "© Prometric")

celpip.ca blocks the fetch tool (HTTP 403), so all CELPIP figures below are **[S]**: taken from search extracts of the July 2026 Test-Taker Guidebook, the 2026 Overview, Listening, Reading and Speaking study packs, the 2023 Performance Standards PDF and the official test-format and score pages. **Re-check them on https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-format/ before publishing.**

**Overall:** about 3 hours in one sitting, computer-delivered, in this order: Listening → Reading → Writing → Speaking. The **CELPIP-General LS** variant (Listening and Speaking only) is used for citizenship **[U]**.

| Component | Time (2026 guidebook) | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | 46–55 min | Unscored practice task + 6 parts, **38 scored questions** |
| Reading | 43–56 min | Unscored practice task + 4 parts, **38 scored questions** |
| Writing | 53 min | 2 tasks |
| Speaking | about 15 min (older editions: 15–20) | Unscored practice task + 8 tasks |

Older (2025) guidebooks gave Listening 47–55, Reading 55–60, Writing 53–60 and Speaking 15–20 minutes. Use the 2026 figures.

#### Listening

| Part | Name | Questions | Audio type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listening to Problem Solving | 8 [S] | Conversation in sections, with an image of the setting [U] |
| 2 | Listening to a Daily Life Conversation | 5 [S] | Everyday conversation [U] |
| 3 | Listening for Information | 6 [S] | Conversation/interview giving information [U] |
| 4 | Listening to a News Item | 5 (inferred from the 38 total) [U] | News report [U] |

| 6 | Listening to Viewpoints | 6 [U] | Talk or report presenting viewpoints [U] |

Questions are multiple choice, mostly in drop-down menus. Some Part 1 items use image options [U]. Audio plays once.

#### Reading

| Part | Name | Questions | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reading Correspondence | 11 | A letter or email with MCQs, then a reply with **drop-down sentence completion** [S/U] |
| 2 | Reading to Apply a Diagram | 8 | A **diagram/chart/schedule** plus an email with drop-down blanks, and MCQs [S/U] |
| 3 | Reading for Information | 9 | Paragraphs A–D; match statements to a paragraph, with an "E = not given" option [U] |
| 4 | Reading for Viewpoints | 10 | Article with MCQs, then a reader's comment with drop-down completion [U] |

Per-part times in third-party sources (about 11/9/10/13 min) are **[U]**.

#### Scoring

- Each component gets a **CELPIP level**. Current scale: **12 down to 0, plus NA** [S].
- Older reports used **"M"** (minimal proficiency / insufficient information to assess). The 2024 CELPIP data report says levels 0–2 were previously reported as M [S].
- **NA** means the component was not administered [S].
- Speaking is rated by 3–5 raters and Writing by 4–6 raters [S] (https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/).

**CELPIP to CLB mapping** [S] (https://www.celpip.ca/score-comparison-chart/; IRCC [V] lists CELPIP level = CLB level for CLB 4–10+):

| CELPIP level | CLB |
|---|---|
| 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3 | Same number (12…3) |
| 2 | CLB 1–2 |
| 1, 0 | No CLB (insufficient information to assess) |
| NA | Not assessed |

**Performance-standard dimensions (names only)** [S]. Source: https://www.celpip.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/CELPIP-Performance-Standards.pdf

| Writing | Speaking |
|---|---|
| Content/Coherence | Content/Coherence |
| Vocabulary | Vocabulary |
| **Readability** (format/paragraphing, connectors, grammar, spelling and punctuation) | **Listenability** (rhythm/pronunciation/intonation, pauses, grammar) |
| Task Fulfillment (relevance, completeness, tone, **word count**) | Task Fulfillment (relevance, completeness, tone, **length**) |

**2025/2026 changes:** no structural change to CELPIP was found. Only the published section durations were revised (see above). A Careers360 claim that CELPIP was "replaced by CAEL" contradicts the 2026 official materials and is **false/unreliable**.

## Part C: Openly licensed content we could legally INCLUDE

### C1. Open PTE / CELPIP question banks: none usable

- GitHub search (`gh search repos`, Nov 2026) for "PTE academic", "pte core", "celpip" and "pte questions" returned **no repository with an open-licensed question bank of clear provenance**.
  - Most repos have **no licence**, which means all rights are reserved.
  - A few are MIT or Apache-licensed, e.g. `alansignetti/pte-simulator` (MIT), `keremgurel/celpip-coach` (MIT) and `lorenz-liu/pte-core` (Apache-2.0). These licences cover **code**. Any embedded questions may be copied from Pearson or Paragon, so the licence cannot be trusted for content.
  - Repos and sites offering "prediction", "real exam" or "memory" questions (e.g. `hongquyen196/pte-question-bank`, `RatnamDubey/CELPIP`, `hustwjw-code/awesome-pte`, and Scribd/Issuu dumps) are **UNUSABLE**. They are likely copied from official materials or recalled from live tests, which may breach test-taker agreements.
- No PTE or CELPIP question banks were found on OER Commons, MERLOT, BCcampus or eCampusOntario [U, not exhaustively searched]. OpenExamPrep (https://open-exam-prep.com) claims "exam-style, not official" items, but no open licence was confirmed [U]. Do not import.
- **Conclusion:** write original items in-house. Build them on the openly licensed sources below.

### C3. Practical licensing notes

1. **Prefer CC BY / PD sources** (OWID, NASA, StatCan, OpenStax, VOA-original, LibriVox, Tatoeba text) for anything we sell.
   - CC BY-SA (NGSL/NAWL, Wikipedia) is usable commercially, but derived assets must stay CC BY-SA. Keep such assets in separate, clearly labelled files.
   - CC BY-NC materials can be used only if the site is non-commercial.
2. Keep an **attribution register**: a table per asset with source URL, licence, author and date retrieved.
3. Never use **Pearson or Paragon question content, audio, images or sample responses**. Link to them instead. Do not use their logos. Treat "PTE" and "CELPIP" as trademarks: use them only descriptively and add a "not affiliated" disclaimer.
4. Pearson's paid-prep T&Cs: https://www.pearsonpte.com/content/dam/ELL/pte/pearsonpte/pdfs/term-and-conditions-pte-paid-preparation.pdf
